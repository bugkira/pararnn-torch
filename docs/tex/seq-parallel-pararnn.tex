\begin{abstract}
The informal claim ``Para-sLSTM can train on many GPUs, FlashRNN cannot'' is
\textbf{false} for data-parallel SGD: both methods split the batch. The true
distinction is \emph{sequence} (time) parallelism of the recurrence itself.
Newton+scan evaluates the cell on a guessed trajectory in parallel across
time and solves the linearized system with an associative monoid of span
$O(\log T)$. A sequential kernel such as FlashRNN's sLSTM has span $\Theta(T)$
along time: rank $r{+}1$ cannot start token $t_{\mathrm{mid}}$ until rank $r$'s
last hidden state exists. This note states both facts; it is not a complexity
proof that FlashRNN is unusable on a cluster.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}

A nonlinear RNN is $h_t = f(h_{t-1}, x_t)$ with $h_{-1}$ given (paper:
Danieli et al., ParaRNN, arXiv:2510.21450). Training a minibatch of sequences
of length $T$ with batch $B$ and hidden width $d$.

\textbf{FlashRNN} (NX-AI) implements sLSTM as a sequential fused kernel along
$t$. \textbf{Para-sLSTM} in this repo is the same cell class (Beck et al.\
xLSTM) trained with Newton on $F(H)_t = h_t - f(h_{t-1}, x_t)=0$ (Alg.~1),
including $\mathrm{mix}=\mathrm{head}$ (dense $R$ inside a head; Jacobian
$4d_{\mathrm{head}}\times 4d_{\mathrm{head}}$ per head). Fused Triton in this
repo is $\mathrm{mix}=\mathrm{diag}$ only.

\section{Data parallel (batch): both methods}

\begin{proposition}[DDP / batch split]
\label{prop:ddp}
Let $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B} \ell(H^{(b)}, x^{(b)})$.
Partitioning $\{1,\ldots,B\}$ across $R$ ranks and averaging gradients is
valid for any $f$ that is independent across batch index $b$, including a
sequential sLSTM kernel and a Newton solve.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The map $b \mapsto (H^{(b)}, \nabla_\theta \ell^{(b)})$ does not depend on
$H^{(b')}$ for $b'\neq b$. FlashRNN's sequential loop is inside one sequence,
not across the batch. Therefore $R$ GPUs with $B/R$ sequences each implement
standard data-parallel SGD for both backends.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The claim ``FlashRNN cannot train on many cards'' is therefore false as
stated. Clusters train sequential LSTMs with DDP every day.
\end{remark}

\section{Sequence parallel: sequential recurrence}

Split time into tiles $I_r = \{t_r,\ldots,t_{r+1}-1\}$ on rank $r$, with
$t_0=0$, $t_R=T$.

\begin{proposition}[Carry dependence of $f$]
\label{prop:seq}
For $h_t = f(h_{t-1}, x_t)$, the first state on tile $r{+}1$ is
$h_{t_{r+1}} = f(h_{t_{r+1}-1}, x_{t_{r+1}})$. Computing it requires
$h_{t_{r+1}-1}$, which lives on rank $r$. No amount of local work on rank
$r{+}1$ removes that edge. The span of a chain of $T$ such steps is
$\Theta(T)$ (constant work per step, unit communication of $h$ per cut).
\end{proposition}

This is the FlashRNN / BPTT picture: sequence-pipeline parallelism exists
(GPipe-style), but the \emph{recurrence along $t$} stays a chain. Rank
$r{+}1$ waits.

\section{Newton: residual is parallel given a guess}

Newton iterates a trajectory $H^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times d}$ (batch omitted).
The residual
\[
F(H)_t = h_t - f(h_{t-1}, x_t)
\]
at a \emph{fixed} $H$ uses $h_{t-1}$ from the guess, not from the sequential
orbit of $f$ starting at $h_{-1}$. Evaluating $F$ (and the Jacobian
$J_t = \partial f/\partial h$ at that guess) is therefore independent across
$t$ given $H$ and $x$. That is the training-time distinction: the cell is no
longer a $\Theta(T)$ loop.

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise residual]
\label{prop:F}
Given $H$ stored (or sharded) across time, $F(H)_t$ and $J_t$ at each $t$
may be computed with no wait on $F(H)_{t'}$ for $t'\neq t$. Communication is
only whatever is needed to read $h_{t-1}$ if $H$ is sharded (one halo of
width $1$).
\end{proposition}

\section[Associative scan]{Associative scan: $O(\log T)$ span, two-rank carry}

The Newton step solves $\delta_t = J_t \delta_{t-1} + r_t$ with
$\delta_{<0}=0$ (paper eq.~2.4). Pairs $(J,r)$ form a monoid
\[
(J_r, r_r)\oplus(J_l, r_l) = (J_r J_l,\; J_r r_l + r_r)
\]
(associative; identity $(I,0)$). Blelloch scan has work $O(T)$ times the
cost of $\oplus$ and span $O(\log T)$ on a PRAM (Blelloch 1990; DeepPCR /
ParaRNN).

\begin{proposition}[Two-tile carry]
\label{prop:twotile}
Let $m=\lfloor T/2\rfloor$. Let $\delta^{L}$ be the inclusive scan of the
left tile (zero left state). Let $\delta^{R,0}$ be the inclusive scan of the
right tile from zero, and $P_t = J_{m+t}\cdots J_m$ the inclusive Jacobian
products on the right. Then the global scan on the right tile is
\[
\delta_{m+t} = P_t\,\delta_{m-1} + \delta^{R,0}_t.
\]
The two local scans do not depend on each other. After they finish, the only
cross-rank datum is $\delta_{m-1}$ (size $O(Bd)$ for diagonal $J$, or
$O(B(4d_{\mathrm{head}}))$ per head for $\mathrm{mix}=\mathrm{head}$ after
folding heads into the batch of a dense scan).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Induction on $t$ in the right tile. Base: $\delta_m = J_m \delta_{m-1}+r_m$
and $\delta^{R,0}_0 = r_m$, $P_0=J_m$. Step: $\delta_{m+t}=J_{m+t}\delta_{m+t-1}+r_{m+t}$.
\end{proof}

Head mix does not change the monoid: $\oplus$ is matrix multiply of size
$4d_{\mathrm{head}}$. Across heads the Jacobian is block-diagonal, so heads
are an extra batch axis (this repo's $\mathrm{scan\_dense}$ packing).

\section{What this does \emph{not} prove}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Wall-clock speedup on one GPU.} Overlapping two CUDA streams
        on a saturated 2080~Ti is a \emph{correctness} model of two ranks,
        not a weak-scaling experiment. Expect little or no speedup; see
        \texttt{docs/seq-parallel-report.md}.
  \item \textbf{That FlashRNN cannot use $R>1$ GPUs.} Proposition~\ref{prop:ddp}.
  \item \textbf{Weak scaling of fused Para-sLSTM head kernels.} This repo has
        no fused $\mathrm{mix}=\mathrm{head}$ kernel; the scan is still the
        same monoid.
  \item \textbf{Pipeline vs tensor parallel of $W_x$.} GEMMs shard as usual;
        orthogonal to the recurrence.
\end{itemize}
